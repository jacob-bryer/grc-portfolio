# Manufacturing — NIST CSF Gap Assessment

## Scenario
Midwest Precision Parts is a fictional small manufacturer that makes custom metal components for commercial customers. The company has about 85 employees and runs on a mix of office systems, production workstations, shared file storage, email, vendor portals, and a handful of cloud applications.

The company has grown fast, but nobody's ever sat down and formally reviewed its security program. Leadership is starting to worry about the obvious stuff — ransomware, production downtime, people having access they shouldn't, documentation that doesn't really exist, and whether they could actually recover if something went wrong.

## Objective
I evaluated the company's current cybersecurity practices against the NIST Cybersecurity Framework to find the gaps that actually matter to the business, not just the ones that look bad on paper. The goal was to turn a framework review into something leadership could read, understand, and act on — not a 40-page report that gets filed away and forgotten.

## Scope
This assessment covers:
- Identity and access management
- Asset inventory
- Data protection
- Endpoint security
- Email and phishing defenses
- Backup and recovery
- Incident response readiness
- Vendor and third-party risk
- Security awareness training
- Policies and procedures

Operational technology, industrial control systems, and physical plant equipment were out of scope.

## Assumptions
- Findings are based on the fictional environment and stakeholder representations described above, not on technical testing.
- No vulnerability scanning or penetration testing was performed as part of this scenario.
- Current state reflects a single point in time and assumes no changes were made during the engagement.

## Deliverables
This folder contains:
- **NIST-CSF-Gap-Analysis.xlsx** — control gap assessment mapped to NIST CSF functions and categories
- **Remediation-Roadmap.xlsx** — prioritized action plan that turns the identified gaps into realistic remediation steps

## Methodology
1. Review the business environment and assumptions
2. Compare current practices against NIST CSF categories
3. Identify gaps across governance, protection, detection, response, and recovery
4. Rate each gap by business impact and urgency
5. Recommend remediation steps that are realistic for a company this size
6. Organize the recommendations into a phased roadmap

## Key Risk Themes
- Limited asset inventory
- Inconsistent user access reviews
- Backup processes that aren't regularly tested
- No formal incident response plan
- Limited phishing and security awareness training
- No documented vendor risk review process
- No clear ownership of cybersecurity responsibilities

## Disclaimer
Midwest Precision Parts is a fictional company created for portfolio purposes. All findings, risks, and recommendations in this folder are fictional and don't represent any real company or client.
