# Retail — Cyber Insurance Readiness

## Scenario
Northside Home Goods is a fictional small retail business that sells home products through a physical storefront and a basic e-commerce site. The company has about 40 employees across sales, operations, customer service, inventory, and management.

The business processes customer orders, accepts card payments, uses email for vendor communication, keeps business records in cloud applications, and leans on third-party providers for payment processing, website hosting, payroll, and point-of-sale. Leadership is preparing to apply for cyber insurance and wants to know whether their current security practices would actually hold up to underwriting scrutiny. They're also worried about the usual small-business nightmares — ransomware, business interruption, phishing, payment fraud, and not having a plan if customer or business data gets exposed.

## Objective
I evaluated the company's cyber insurance readiness and identified the security gaps most likely to affect coverage eligibility, premiums, exclusions, or how a claim would actually play out. This scenario focuses on practical control review, documenting weaknesses honestly, and building the policies and response procedures a small business actually needs — not just checking boxes for an application.

## Scope
This assessment covers:
- Multi-factor authentication
- Email security and phishing protection
- Endpoint protection
- Backup and recovery
- Incident response planning
- Acceptable use expectations
- Administrative access controls
- Vendor and third-party risk
- Payment and customer data handling
- Security awareness training
- Business continuity considerations

Out of scope: legal review of an insurance policy, policy pricing, claim negotiation, PCI DSS certification, and technical penetration testing.

## Assumptions
- Findings are based on the fictional environment and stakeholder representations described above, not on technical testing.
- No vulnerability scanning, penetration testing, or formal PCI assessment was performed as part of this scenario.
- Readiness expectations reflect common underwriting trends at the time of writing, not the requirements of any specific insurer.

## Deliverables
This folder contains:
- **Insurance-Readiness-Report.pdf** — written review of cyber insurance readiness, key gaps, business risk, and recommended improvements
- **Policies/**
  - **Acceptable-Use-Policy.pdf** — sample employee technology use policy for a small retail environment
  - **Incident-Response-Plan.pdf** — sample incident response plan covering preparation, detection, escalation, containment, communication, recovery, and post-incident review

## Methodology
1. Review the fictional retail environment and assumptions
2. Identify systems, vendors, and processes that create cyber risk
3. Compare current practices against common cyber insurance readiness expectations
4. Document gaps that could increase the likelihood or impact of a cyber incident
5. Prioritize recommendations based on risk reduction and what's realistic for a business this size
6. Build out the supporting policy and incident response documentation

## Key Risk Themes
- Inconsistent multi-factor authentication
- Limited incident response documentation
- Weak employee acceptable use expectations
- Backup recovery that isn't regularly tested
- Heavy reliance on third-party vendors
- Limited review of privileged accounts
- Phishing and business email compromise risk
- No formal security awareness training
